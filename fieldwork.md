## Documentation as methodology

My fieldwork combines **naturalistic audiovisual recording, targeted grammatical elicitation, lexical work, sociolinguistic interviews, procedural documentation, oral history, and cultural documentation**. I use these materials to build annotated corpora, grammatical descriptions, lexical resources, archival collections, and speech datasets.

My current major field project focuses on **Uneme**, with documentation across Uneme-speaking communities in Nigeria. I have also conducted linguistic research involving related Edoid languages, including Uhami and Igasi.

The computational dimension of my work grows directly from this process: decisions about speakers, genres, recording conditions, transcription, tone marking, and annotation all shape what a speech model can learn and how its errors should be interpreted.

## What the documentation supports

- annotated and archived audiovisual corpora;
- grammatical description grounded in naturalistic and elicited evidence;
- comparison of language use across speakers, genres, and communities;
- multimedia lexical and bilingual dictionary resources;
- speech-recognition datasets and controlled ASR experiments;
- documentation of oral history, cultural practices, material culture, and indigenous knowledge.

<div class="callout"><strong>Why this matters computationally:</strong> low-resource language technology is often constrained as much by how data is collected, transcribed, annotated, and sampled as by model architecture. Fieldwork therefore forms part of the technical methodology, not merely the source of examples.</div>
